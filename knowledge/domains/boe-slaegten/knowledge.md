## 1. Executive Summary

- Confirmed line: **Peder Christensen Boe** (b. c.1731, d.1810, Sdr. Dråby, Mors) — first documented bearer of the "Boe" surname, per the manuscript's own Jørsby kirkebog citation (1771) — down through four more generations to **Christen Pedersen Boe** (1853-1930) and his nine children, one of whom (Laurids, "g.") alone has eleven children of his own and ~73 descendants.
- The manuscript this domain is built from — "Boe-slægten fra Mors og Fur" (KEJ, maj 2026) — has been read in full: all four chapters, the Anetavler pedigree appendices, and the Kilder bibliography. 363 individuals, 85 families in `family-tree/tree.ged` for this line.
- **This is the family Hanne Boe (KEJ's late ex-wife) belonged to, and — through her — the source of Nikolaj's own "Boe" surname.** KEJ himself appears in his own manuscript as a family member, not only its author (§4).
- The "Boe" surname's origin is explicitly unresolved — KEJ's own comparative research into other Danish "Boe" families found no confirmed connection to any of them (§7).
- The last three-to-four generations are heavily `[SENSITIVE]`/redacted — most of Branch g's descendants, and everyone from Hanne Boe's own generation onward, may be living. Full records: `restricted/tree-sensitive.ged`.
- Several data-quality anomalies in the source manuscript are tracked, not resolved — see `grandfather-review/queue.md` items 4-9 and `family-tree/possible-duplicates.md`.
- One confirmed cross-domain link to Hopp-slægten: Bruno Dølby married into this family in 2000 (§5).

Generation 04 is notable for a documented career beyond farming and fishing: alongside a heath-plot smallholding, Peder Christensen Boe worked as a **gå-post** (walking postman) between Skive and Fur, appointed by the parish priest — a detail preserved via family testimony (Mette Boe, K.K.K. Boe) and corroborated by a 1947 *Skive Folkeblad* article. He died 10.2.1879 falling through Limfjord ice while sledging home; the man he was traveling with was also recovered but Boe himself froze to death. [VERIFIED: boe-slaegten-fra-mors-og-fur, p.18-20]

Generation 05's nine children (labeled a-i in the manuscript) are the branch point for essentially the entire modern descendant tree — see §5.

## 3. The Boe Name — Origin Unknown

KEJ's own text states plainly that the "Boe" name's origin on Mors is not known. Two working theories are offered, neither confirmed: "manden i Boen" (the man at the *bo* — a farm/homestead name) or a derivation from "bonde" (farmer/peasant). A separate, similarly-named "Sallingboe" family in Sønder Dråby was considered and tentatively rejected for lack of any documented link. [VERIFIED: boe-slaegten-fra-mors-og-fur, p.5-8]

KEJ's later, expanded comparative research ("Forskellige Boe-slægter," august 2026) actively tested a connection to the best-documented other Boe family in Denmark (the Fyn line, traced to 1500-1556) and concluded against it. See §7 for the full comparative picture — the net effect of KEJ's own research is that the uncertainty is *strengthened*, not resolved: he has looked, carefully, and found nothing conclusive.

## 4. Notable History

**The "Kyssesekten" (Kiss Sect), generation 02.** A religious sect active in Jørsby, led by Peder Lassen Grud, based on a literal reading of "greet one another with a holy kiss." Peder Lassen fathered children by at least four women, was prosecuted and sentenced to labor at the Copenhagen fortress (later reduced), and continued the sect for some years after release. Peder Christensen Boe's own wife, **Sidsel (Zidsel) Lauridsdatter Toft**, was one of the women involved before their marriage — her son **Mads Pedersen (Boe)** was fathered by Peder Lassen out of wedlock, per what reads as a direct kirkebog transcription ("blef udlagt til Barne Fader"). Peder Boe himself fell away from the sect in 1773 and testified against Peder Lassen. Mads later took the "Boe" surname from his stepfather; descendants still carried it as of 2019 per the manuscript. [VERIFIED: boe-slaegten-fra-mors-og-fur, p.5-8]

**The 1904/05 royal name grant, generation 05.** "Boe" had long functioned only as an informal kaldenavn, not a legal surname — both Peder Christensen Boe (04) and Christen Pedersen Boe (05) were called "Peder-"/"Christen Boe" in daily speech without it being their registered name. Securing the legal right to it required a *kongelig bevilling* (royal permission), sought c.1904/05 apparently at son Laurids's urging. Not everyone was included: eldest son Jens Christian ("a.") had already emigrated and lost contact; Jens Peder ("c.") refused; daughter Else Marie ("i.") had died by then. Six of the nine siblings received the name by bevilling in 1905. [VERIFIED: boe-slaegten-fra-mors-og-fur, p.21-24]

**Mette Boe's Legat.** Mette Christensen Boe ("f.", 1884-1977) worked over 25 years as housekeeper to a Nykøbing Mors lawyer, inherited his fortune, and by testament (1972, fundats 1977) established a 70,000 kr. charitable trust distributing annually — on her own birthday where possible — to a woman born on or strongly connected to Mors, "for help and encouragement in the recipient's recreation." Still distributing as of 1998 per the manuscript. [VERIFIED: boe-slaegten-fra-mors-og-fur, p.25-54]

**KEJ himself appears in his own manuscript, not only as its author.** Generation 05's child "g." (Laurids Christensen Boe) has a granddaughter, **Hanne Boe/Nielsen** (b.1944, d.2005) — born out of wedlock (declared father Gunnar Vilhelm Carlsen; the fuller account of this is restricted, see §9), later legally "Boe" — who married **Knud Erik Jakobsen** in 1966 (dissolved 1983). This is squarely why KEJ compiled this manuscript in the first place, not a coincidental overlap. Their two daughters and all documented descendants down to two great-great-grandchildren born 2023-2025 are recorded in the same passage. See §5's branch-g detail. [VERIFIED: boe-slaegten-fra-mors-og-fur, p.25-54]

## 5. Descendant Branches (Efterslægt)

Generation 05's nine children, and what became of each line. Six are short and closed (no further descendants, or the branch simply ends); three are large enough to have their own extensive descendant trees.

|---|---|---|

| b. | Marie Christensen Boe | Son Esper Marinus (b.1897, out of wedlock, legitimized) raised by his grandfather, later a master mason; married Niels Jensen (1910), 3 more children. |
| c. | Jens Peter Christensen | Shoe manufacturer, Odense. No biological children (an adoptive daughter). Refused the 1905 name grant. |

| e. | Jens Christensen Boe | Farmer, Kønsberg, Fur. Four children, including **Christen Knudsen Christensen Boe ("K.K.K. Boe")** — a teacher whose 1976 oral-history account is this manuscript's main secondhand source for generation 04-05's family lore. |

## 6. Ancestor Lines Behind In-Marrying Spouses

Two of the manuscript's four chapters aren't new descendant branches — they trace the ancestry of spouses generation 02-03 married into the family:

- **Chapter 2 — the Toft family**: Sidsel Lauridsdatter Toft's (02's wife) own father, siblings, and their descendant lines. KEJ flags this chapter's own source character directly: **"Toft-familiens familierelationer kan være noget komplicerede! Efterslægten kan for nogle børn være noget usikker!"** ("The Toft family's relationships can be somewhat complicated! The descendancy may be somewhat uncertain for some children!") — treat child-ordering claims in this chapter with correspondingly less confidence. Everyone in this chapter died well over a century ago; no `[SENSITIVE]` handling needed. [VERIFIED: boe-slaegten-fra-mors-og-fur, p.55-65]
- **Chapter 3 — the Møller family**: Mette Jensdatter's (03's wife) own father, a miller's son orphaned in infancy (both parents died within weeks of each other, 1743), traced two more generations back. More uncertain still than Chapter 2 — the source's own section headings for the two oldest generations carry a literal question mark, reflecting KEJ's doubt about distinguishing same-named contemporaries. [VERIFIED: boe-slaegten-fra-mors-og-fur, p.66-73]
- **Chapter 4** is a single page, entirely a cross-reference back to Chapter 3 — no new individuals. [VERIFIED: boe-slaegten-fra-mors-og-fur, p.74]
- **The Anetavler appendices** (Søgaard-slægten, Lundgaard-slægten/Fur-slægter, Degn-slægten Frammerslev/Fur) trace three more in-marrying spouses' ancestries — **exist only as hand-drawn pedigree charts**, not prose text, discovered when the plain-text extraction turned out to contain nothing for them beyond table-of-contents entries. Only the direct connecting lines were modeled in `tree.ged`; several generations of deeper 17th-century material (documented from as early as 1590/1614) were deliberately left unmined — available in the media folder, not silently dropped. [VERIFIED: boe-slaegten-fra-mors-og-fur, Anetavler p.75-91]

## 7. Comparative "Boe" Research — Other, Unconnected Families

KEJ's own manuscript (p.86-91) and his later expanded compilation ("Forskellige Boe-slægter," august 2026) survey other Danish families carrying the "Boe"/"Bomand" name, explicitly to test — and, in every case but one, rule out — a connection to this line:

| Family | Location/Period | KEJ's conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Boe på Mors | Nykøbing, 1690-1786 | No connection provable |
| Boe på Ålborgegnen | Ålborg, early 1700s, merchant class | Different social class/location argue against a link |
| **Boe i Himmerland/Thy** | Sebbersund/Nibe area | **Plausible but unconfirmed** — a shared noble estate owner (Sivert Brockenhuus) connects Mors and this area; a specific candidate connecting individual (Anders Pedersen Boe, Vorring) was later named in the expanded 2026 compilation. The one genuinely open lead — see `grandfather-review/queue.md` item 17. |
| Boe i Sønderjylland/Slesvig | Seem/Spandet, 1700s-1800s | No connection claimed |
| Fyn-slægten Boe | Traced to 1500-1556, published source ("Boernes Slægtsbog," 1960) | **Explicitly ruled out** by KEJ himself: "Det må derimod anses for usandsynligt..." |
| Bomand / Boe (general) | Viborg, Sjælland, etc. | Reinforces that the name isn't unique enough to imply shared origin |
| Boe i Nyord ved Møn | Early 1800s, skippers/pilots | No connection claimed |

No individuals from this comparative research were added to `family-tree/tree.ged` — doing so would fabricate relationship links KEJ himself says can't be shown. [VERIFIED: boe-slaegten-fra-mors-og-fur, Boe-slægter p.86-91][VERIFIED: forskellige-boe-slaegter]

## 8. Known Data-Quality Issues

The source manuscript itself contains internal date/age inconsistencies, tracked per this repo's correction discipline (transcribed and flagged, never silently corrected) rather than resolved here:

- Mads Pedersen (Boe)'s birth year is internally inconsistent across three separate mentions in the same manuscript (1774, 1773, and three different implied years from age-at-record statements) — `grandfather-review/queue.md` item 4.
- Else Marie Christensen's (generation 05's youngest child) death year is genuinely contradictory within the source itself — one passage gives an infant death (1894), another implies she lived to c.1900 — item 5.
- Two separate fadder-labeling mismatches (a person correctly named but with the wrong relationship/branch-numeral) escalated to review after crossing this project's 3-instance reassessment threshold — items 6, 7. Both were substantially outweighed by later checkpoints turning up many *correctly*-labeled fadder references in the same branch (eleven correct vs. three incorrect, as of the last count) — reads as localized slips, not a systemic numbering problem.
- Margit Bak's death date, as given, predates her own birth by 35 years — clearly wrong, left blank in `tree.ged` rather than guessed — item 8.
- The Anders Pedersen Boe (Himmerland/Thy) connection — item 17, see §7 above.

Full running list, including several lower-confidence anomalies not escalated: `family-tree/possible-duplicates.md`.
