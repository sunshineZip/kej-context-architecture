# Working Hypothesis — Hopp-Slægten Before Knud Danielsen Hopp (pre-1640)

Version 1.1 | 2026-08-09 | Active

---

## Document Purpose

This family's own hypothesis about the generations proposed *before* Knud Danielsen Hopp (b. ~1640) — the earliest ancestor currently confirmed via KEJ's own material. Distinct from `external-source-hopp-herredsfoged.md` and `external-source-leunbach-anetavle.md`, which preserve what two external sources claim; this file is Nikolaj's own synthesis across both of them, developed in conversation on 2026-08-09, not something either source states outright. Not yet shown to KEJ — he may well have reached the same conclusions independently and simply never written them down; see the open question in `grandfather-review/queue.md` item 2.

## The Claimed Chain

```
Hans Hop              b. 1562, d. 1624 — Metzingen (Schwartzwald, Württemberg) → later Holland
   |  (father of, per "Slægten Hop," 1989 — uncited)
Mads (Mathias) Hop     b. 1590, d. 1650 — immigrated to Skagen c. 1625; købmand, later rådmand
   |  (father of, per same uncited source)
Daniel Madsen Hopp     birth year disputed — see Hypothesis A below
   |  (father of — this step is what the "Danielsen" patronymic below rests on)
Knud Danielsen Hopp    b. ~1640 — CONFIRMED root ancestor, family's own "chapter 01"
```

Everything from Knud Danielsen Hopp downward (his son Daniel, grandson Niels, and on to KEJ and Nikolaj) is independently confirmed via KEJ's own chapters, sourced to church books and probate records. Everything above Knud rests entirely on one uncited secondary source (the 1989 book "Slægten Hop," relayed via two later web/PDF compilations — `external-source-hopp-herredsfoged.md` and `external-source-leunbach-anetavle.md`).

## Hypothesis 1 — Mads Hop as the regional surname founder

**The claim:** Mads Hop, arriving in Skagen c. 1625, is the single common ancestor behind *multiple* documented Hop/Hopp branches later found across North and Central Jutland — not just Daniel Madsen Hopp's line (→ Knud Danielsen Hopp → this family), but also a second, "ret udbredt" (fairly widespread) branch via another proposed son, Hans Madsen Hop (rådmand i Skagen, 1671), whose own descendants spread through Tornby, Tøndering, Thy, and down to Irup and Svansø near Skive.

**Why this is worth taking seriously:** a single immigrant founder whose several sons each start a sub-line, spreading a distinctive surname through a region over two or three generations, is a common and plausible pattern — it isn't competing evidence against Mads Hop's role, it's a mechanism that would explain it.

**Why it isn't more than a hypothesis:** both branches (Daniel's and Hans's) trace to Mads Hop via the *same* single uncited source. This isn't two independent researchers converging on the same answer — it's one unverified narrative being repeated across two later compilations of the same book. Nothing here has been checked against a primary record (kirkebog, skifte) naming either parent-child link directly.

## Hypothesis A — The 1633 birth year for Daniel Madsen Hopp is a recording error

**The problem:** the Leunbach anetavle (Svend Aage Nielsen, 2021, citing the 1989 "Slægten Hop" book) gives Daniel Madsen Hopp's birth as 1633. Knud Danielsen Hopp, his claimed son, was born ~1640 — seven years later. A father cannot be seven years old. One of the two dates, or the parent-child claim itself, has to be wrong.

**This isn't only Nikolaj's idea — it's already half-proposed in the earlier source.** The akj-cbj.dk page (`external-source-hopp-herredsfoged.md`) shows the same tension: its own summary chart lists Daniel Madsen Hopp's birth as "omk 1620" (already silently adjusted to something workable), while its prose separately reports the book's stated 1633 and flags it as probably wrong, suggesting Daniel was "eventuelt født før slægten indvandrede til Skagen omkring år 1625" (possibly born before the family emigrated to Skagen c. 1625). That adjustment was never itself sourced either — it's a guess layered on a guess, just an older one.

**What would actually resolve this:** a primary record giving Daniel Madsen Hopp's real birth year and/or naming his father directly. Two candidate search areas, based on where the family is actually placed by the sources (not Zealand/Sjælland — nothing in either source puts this family there at any point):

- **If Daniel was born after the family's arrival in Skagen (c. 1625 onward):** Skagen or wider Vendsyssel kirkebøger for the 1620s-30s.
- **If Daniel was born before the move (per the "born earlier" theory):** the family's whereabouts immediately before Skagen aren't precisely dated in either source — Hans Hop is described as moving from Metzingen to Holland at some unspecified point, and Mads Hop (b. 1590) may have grown up in either place before emigrating himself c. 1625. Dutch civil/church records c. 1615-1625 would be the candidate, if a specific town can be pinned down — currently unknown.
- **The single most decisive record, if it survives:** Knud Danielsen Hopp's own baptism entry, ~1640, most plausibly in the Aalborg area (per his claimed birthplace in `external-source-hopp-herredsfoged.md` — itself uncorroborated). A baptism record from this period often names the father directly, which would settle the "Daniel" question without needing Daniel's own birth year resolved at all.

Logged to `research-queue.md` as an archive-lookup candidate, since resolving this needs primary-record access rather than more secondary-source comparison.

## Hypothesis B — DISCARDED (2026-08-09) — Knud Danielsen Hopp as Mads Hop's direct son

**Discarded by Nikolaj after reviewing the patronymic argument below.** Kept here, not deleted, so the reasoning stays on record — same convention as other ruled-out leads elsewhere in this project (e.g. the Nykøbing Mors Boe hypothesis in `forskellige-boe-slaegter-extraction.md`).

**The idea, as raised in conversation:** skip Daniel Madsen Hopp entirely — what if Knud Danielsen Hopp was Mads Hop's own son, not grandson, perhaps via a younger second wife? Mads (b. 1590) would have been about 50 at Knud's birth (~1640) — biologically unremarkable for a father, unlike Hypothesis A's outright impossibility.

**Why it was discarded:** every source that names Knud Danielsen Hopp's patronymic — including `family-tree/tree.ged#I364` itself, drawn from KEJ's own chapter "01" overview table, independently of either external source — gives it consistently as **"Danielsen."** In this period, Danish patronymics were formed directly and specifically from the father's given name; "Danielsen" is only expected if the father's own first name was Daniel. Three independent-of-each-other sources (KEJ's own material, plus the two external compilations) agreeing on "Danielsen" is a real, if not conclusive, piece of evidence that a father actually named Daniel existed — which Hypothesis A accounts for cleanly (Daniel exists, his birth year is just wrong) and this hypothesis would have to explain away (why would a son of Mads be recorded everywhere as "Danielsen"?). Rescuing it would require inventing an unrecorded middle name "Daniel" for Mads himself, or some unevidenced naming irregularity — a bigger leap for no real gain over Hypothesis A.

**Also unsupported on its own terms:** neither source mentions a wife for Mads Hop at all, let alone a second one — the "younger second wife" detail was pure speculation with nothing to anchor it, on top of the naming problem above.
