## Bomand/Boe — name origin (p.2)

Background research on "Bomand" as a name attested across Denmark well before 1735 (when the Fyn line's own usage shifts from Bomand to Boe), independent of any one family. Examples given: a "Bomand"/"Bokone" pairing at "Langøersbo" on Helnæs as early as 1599-1600 (tied to the Fyn-slægten below); a 1665 memorial inscription in Viborg cathedral naming a "Maria Bomand"; Cathrine Justdatter Boemand, wife of a Jebjerg (Salling) parish priest 1645-57, buried there 1705 — Jebjerg is in Salling, the same general region as the Mors/Fur line, but KEJ draws no connection and neither is one asserted here; a Sørbymagle/Kirkerup (Sjælland) Bomand family, Jens Andersen Bomand (o.1575-o.1630) with 8 children, entirely unconnected geographically. KEJ's own summary: the Bomand name is already widespread across most of Denmark by the 1500s, so its presence in any one family's background carries little evidentiary weight on its own — reinforcing, not narrowing, the open "Boe surname origin" question already on record from the original manuscript's own comparative section.

## Boe på Mors (p.3) — Nykøbing Mors case, now ruled out with specifics

Covers a Christen (Nielsen) Boe of Nykøbing Mors itself, b. o.1690 (per burial record, "76 år" at death 10.4.1786 — so actually b. o.1710, not the TOC's stated "1690"; KEJ's own body text uses the later year, the TOC entry appears to be a rounding/typo). The original manuscript's own comparative section (p.86-91) had already flagged this exact family — this document adds the specific probate evidence that settles it.

Christen Nielsen Boe's first wife, Mette Nielsdatter (b. o.1707, buried 19.11.1777), left, per the probate opened 12.2.1778, **only two children**: son **Niels Christensen Boe** (b. o.1759, apprentice cabinetmaker in Aalborg at the time, later a shoemaker's journeyman in Nykøbing) and daughter **Kirsten Christensdatter** (b. o.1752, unmarried, remained in the household — noted as owner of the house's stove per the probate). Christen Nielsen Boe remarried (Kiernstine/Chirsten Chrestensdatter) and died 10.4.1786, 76 years old; the second probate (11.4.1786) confirms the same two children as the only heirs of the first marriage, with detailed household/debt accounting (house on Østergade valued at 6-10 rigsdaler, a 9 rdl. running account with a local merchant including funeral costs itemized down to coffee, brandy, and beer).

KEJ's own conclusion, quoted directly: **"Et eventuelt slægtsforhold mellem Jens og Peder Christensen Boe og denne Christen Nielsen Boe kan p.t. ikke påvises. Ej heller til andre kendte Boe-slægter."** ("A possible family relationship between Jens and Peder Christensen Boe and this Christen Nielsen Boe cannot currently be shown. Nor to other known Boe families.") He repeats the same naming theory as the original manuscript — "Boe" plausibly from "manden i Boen" (the man at the Bo[e]/farmstead) or simply "bonde" (farmer) — not a shared-ancestor theory.

Two children documented individually in the source (Kirsten Christensdatter, Niels Christensen Boe) — not modeled in `tree.ged`, per the no-fabricated-links policy above.

## Boe på Ålborgegnen (p.4-5)

A merchant-class Boe family in Ålborg, early-to-mid 1700s, centered on **Jens Clausen Boe** (b. 26.9.1731, Ålborg Budolfi; d. 17.9.1801, same parish) — son of Claus Jensen Boved (the source's own spelling, likely a scribal variant), klokker (sexton/bell-ringer) at Budolfi kirke, described as "en god naturhistoriker, numismatiker – og formuende" (a good naturalist, numismatist — and wealthy). Married Florentine Blume (b. o.1743); documented consistently across the 1787 and 1801 censuses at the same Ålborg address (Aldgaden/Adelgade), with daughters **Mette Cathrine Boe** (b. 20.5.1764, married Knud Christensen Henneberg 1787, d. 1841) and **Nicoline Claudiane Boe** (b. o.1766, still unmarried in the household at 1801) plus a third, **Johanne Marie Boe** (b. 26.5.1768). Also documented: **Hendrikke/Henrikke Boe** (b. o.1727/1729), a separate Ålborg merchant-class Boe, married to byfoged (town bailiff) Chr. Wormstrup/Wernstrup, widowed by 1801; and **Ingeborg Marie Boe**, married 1777 to a Norwegian sergeant. Purely occupational/social contrast material — a town merchant-and-clergy family, geographically and socially distinct from the Mors/Fur farming line — no connection claimed or suggested anywhere in this section.

## Boe i Himmerland/Thy (p.6) — Now with a named candidate link

The original manuscript's own comparative section had already floated an unconfirmed geographic-estate link between the Mors line and a Boe family near Sebbersund; this document develops it further and, for the first time, names a specific plausible connecting individual.

The starting point is a shared noble estate history: in 1667 Sivert Brockenhuus til Ullerup på Mors (d.1688) inherited Sebberkloster, near Sebbersund in Himmerland — the same Ullerup that is `family-tree/tree.ged#I4`'s (Christen Pedersen Boe, generation "03") own stated employer ("Gårdfæster under Ullerup gods, Jørsby"). KEJ's reasoning: such shared estate ownership sometimes moved tenant-farming families between the landlord's holdings in different regions, so a Sebbersund-area Boe family and the Mors line *could* share this route, though nothing in the record actually shows the move happening.

The Sebbersund/Nibe-area Boe family itself: fishermen, documented via fæsteprotokol entries — **Peder Andersen Boe**, fæstebrev 18.6.1742 (Lundbæk fæsteprotokol) on a house called "Prierboden"; his apparent son or relative **Anders Pedersen Boe**, fæstebreve 21.11.1776 and 9.9.1779 (same protocol) in Diørup; also **Peder Jensen Boe**, fæstebrev 21.4.1736 (Fussingø fæsteprotokol), born in Ålum, possibly a related branch on the Ålum/Hornum side of Himmerland.

**The newly-named candidate link:** KEJ proposes that a possible connecting individual between this Himmerland family and the Mors line could be **Anders Pedersen Boe i Vorring, Nors sogn**, whose probate is dated 28.9.1759, recorded in the Dueholm, Ørum og Vestervig amters skifteprotokol 1749-1759 — i.e., a Boe living in Nors parish, on the Thy/Mors side, at roughly the right period to be a link. KEJ's own framing remains explicitly speculative — "Et evt. bindeled... kunne gå over den Anders Pedersen Boe..." ("A possible connecting link... could go via this Anders Pedersen Boe...") — a proposed avenue for future research, not a demonstrated connection. This is the strongest lead of the whole document but is still unconfirmed.

Also noted: an old rhyming couplet associated with a stone bridge in Stenild sogn ("Tyge Nold og Sivard Boe / Bügde Glenstrup Kirke og Senild Broe"), cited from a 1934 published transcription of a 17th-century source (Peder Hansen Resen's *Atlas Danicus*) — folkloric/onomastic color, not genealogical evidence of a specific family link.

## Boe i Sønderjylland og Slesvig (p.7)

A distinct, reasonably well-documented Boe lineage descending from **Christen Boe** and **Mette Hansdatter** (Mette d.1818) in Seem/Spandet parish, Sønderjylland, spreading through the 1700s-1800s: son **Chresten Christensen Boe** (1783-1838), gårdmand in Faurholt, married Ane Paulsdatter, with at least 6 children including Chresten (b.1820), Poul (b.1822), and Niels (b.1826); **Jens Jepsen C. Boe** (b.1798, Seem), indsidder in Varminglund, whose son **Hans Hermann Christensen Boe** (b.1844) was a styrmand (mate/navigator); also **Jesper Christensen Boe** (b.1800); a separate line from **Jeppe Hansen Boe** of Spandetgårde (son Anders Hansen Boe, b.1825) continuing the Boe name further; **Christen Hansen Boe** (b.1819, Spandet), a husmand around Højrup/Arrild/Spandet and Seem; and a later individual, Christen Boe Georg Christensen Lønberg (b.1878), whose middle names suggest descent from this line via a maternal Boe connection (Cecilie Marie Christensen Boe). KEJ notes the recurring occupational pattern for the Boe name generally — fishing and seafaring — citing **Frands Boe**, a skipper on Rømø in 1732, and Søren Larsen Boe, in Hals on Læsø (per the "Navneregisteret til Kronens Skøder 1731-1765"). No connection to the Mors/Fur line is claimed anywhere in this section; it reads as background on a third, independent Boe lineage.

## Fyn-slægten Boe (p.8) — Explicitly ruled out, same conclusion as before

The best-documented of all the comparative families, per a published genealogy ("Boernes Slægtsbog," 1960), traced back to stamfader **Peder Hansen Bomand** (o.1500-1556), Helnæs, Fyn. This is the same family the original manuscript's own comparative appendix already ruled out ("Det må derimod anses for usandsynligt") — this document repeats the identical conclusion nearly word for word: **"Det må anses som usandsynligt, at der skulle være en forbindelse til den fynske Boe-slægt."** ("It must be considered unlikely that there is a connection to the Funen Boe family.") No new evidence changes this; the section is included for completeness of KEJ's own comparative-surname project, not because a fresh case for connection emerged.

New detail not in the original manuscript's briefer treatment: the family's own four competing internal traditions for its origin — (1) Swedish brothers arriving during the Swedish Wars of the 1600s (rejected by KEJ since the family's own documented history reaches back to 1546, predating the wars); (2) a second, similar Swedish-brothers tradition; (3) Scottish brothers arriving under King Hans (1481-1513); (4) an arrival from Flensborg in Sønderjylland, tied to the "Bomand"/"Busmand"/"Botzman"/"Boesmand" name-variant history traced back toward 1420 — KEJ notes this fourth tradition dovetails with the general "Bomand" background material in the document's own opening section. A 2016 photograph of the "Boe-stenen" (Boe-stone) memorial at Helnæs is the document's one embedded image (`word/media/image1.jpeg`), captioned simply "Boe-stenen på Helnæs 2016" — a monument photo, not a person, not sensitive.

## Boe på Nyord ved Stege på Møn (p.9-22) — Confirmed unconnected, largest section

By far the largest section of the document (14 of its 22 content pages) — a detailed multi-generation genealogy of a fishing/piloting Boe/Bo family on Nyord, a small island (4.99 km²) near Møn, running from the family's start with **Hans Pedersen (Boe/Bo)** (b. 24.10.1753, d. 1831 as "Hans Pedersen Bo," 79 years old — son of "Unge Peder Hansøn" and Maren Rasmusdatter) through roughly five further generations into the 1880s. Hans Pedersen married the widow Ane Kirstine Pedersdatter in 1774 (she was previously married to gårdmand Hans Nielsen, one of the island's 20 gårdmænd who bought their land from the crown in 1769 in exchange for lodspligt, a mandatory piloting-service obligation not lifted until 1879); the couple had 4 children together (labeled a.-d. in KEJ's own lettering), each with substantial further descent documented via parish-register baptisms, census entries (1787, 1801, 1834, 1840, 1845, 1850, 1855, 1860, 1870, 1880), marriages, and deaths — many as lods (harbor pilot), skipper, matros (sailor), or gårdmand, reflecting the island's lodsvæsen (piloting service) economy. KEJ also documents two apparently unconnected individuals bearing the same surname whose relationship to Hans Pedersen's own line he could not establish — "Rasmus Nielsen Boe" (b. o.1760) and "Rasmus Jensen Boe" (b.1765, d.1848) — explicitly flagging "Der er ikke fundet nogen forbindelse mellem Hans Pedersen Boe (f. o.1753) og Rasmus Jensen Boe (f. o.1765)!" (no connection found between the two), i.e. even within this one small island's own records the surname doesn't resolve to a single family.

Surrounding local history (drawn from Wikipedia per KEJ's own citation) on Nyord itself: mentioned in Valdemar Sejr's 13th-century jordebog; part of Jungshoved Len in the early 1600s; refuge for Svend Gjønge during the Swedish Wars (1657-58); crown-owned until sold at auction in 1769 to its own 20 gårdmænd, who took on mandatory piloting duty in exchange; 1763 fire destroyed 15 farms/houses; peak mid-1800s piloting traffic of roughly 3,000 ships/year through the surrounding waters; state took over the pilot service in the 1880s, Nyord Havn built 1888; population peaked around 365 (1901), fell to 41 by 2011, 36 by 2024; became its own parish in 1846, merged back into Stege Landsogn in 1966.

**The key finding, stated explicitly by KEJ and directly relevant to the "our line" question this whole document exists to answer:** the Boe/Bo surname on Nyord dies out locally with the generations documented here. KEJ's own words: **"Ingen 'Boe' eller 'Bo' er fundet nævnt i kirkebog eller folketællinger på Nyord efter 1869!"** ("No 'Boe' or 'Bo' has been found mentioned in the parish register or censuses on Nyord after 1869!") — the last individual specifically noted bearing the surname is Niels Hansen Boe, an aftægtsmand (retired-farmer-on-pension) who died 3.8.1869, 77 years old (his own probable ID, per KEJ's own cross-reference lettering, is "01-d"). No connection to the Mors/Fur line is claimed or plausible here — this is a small, geographically isolated island community whose own "Boe" usage appears to be a local, coincidental adoption of the same generic name pattern noted in the document's opening section (KEJ notes "I 1700-tallet benævnes flere på Møn – også på Nyord – som 'Boe-mand'" — several people on Møn, including Nyord, were called "Boe-mand" in the 1700s, an occupational-sounding nickname rather than an inherited family name in the earliest instances).

Sourced throughout via direct kirkebog quotations (Stege sogn 1765-1859, Nyord sogn 1816-1891) and census entries (Mønbo herred/Stege Landsogn, Stege) rather than a secondary compilation — this is KEJ's own primary-record transcription work, not a copied family history.

None of the individuals in this section are modeled in `tree.ged` — this is a confirmed-unconnected local family, the clearest case in the whole document.

## Net effect on the open "Boe surname origin" question

This document adds specificity to two of the four candidate connections the original manuscript's comparative appendix (p.86-91) had already surveyed, but changes neither conclusion:

1. **Nykøbing Mors (Christen Nielsen Boe):** now conclusively ruled out with named children and a direct probate citation, not just KEJ's earlier general statement.
2. **Himmerland/Thy:** now has a named, dated candidate connecting individual (Anders Pedersen Boe i Vorring, Nors sogn, probate 28.9.1759) — still explicitly speculative, the one genuinely open lead in the whole document.
3. **Sønderjylland/Slesvig and Fyn:** both remain as before — no connection claimed for the former, explicitly ruled unlikely for the latter.
4. **Nyord:** a new section not covered by the original manuscript's own comparative appendix at all — confirmed unconnected, the surname dies out locally after 1869.

Reflected as an added `NOTE` on `family-tree/tree.ged#I1` (Peder Christensen Boe), appended directly after the existing note sourced from the original manuscript's own comparative section, rather than as a separate/duplicate note — this document is KEJ's own expanded revisit of the same question, not a new one.
