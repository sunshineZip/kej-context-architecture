## Checkpoint 1 — Knud Knudsen Hopp himself, his wife, and the 6-child overview (2026-08-08)

Replaced the stopgap stub (`#I384`, `#I385`, `#F127`) with real primary-source content. Knud Knudsen Hopp inherited his parents' fæste in Stouby 29.12.1725 (the fæstebrev itself quoted at length in the source) and was called to the small degneembede in Thise the following year. His 1743 complaint to the bishop is quoted directly: *"...jeg sidder ved et lidet fattigt brød med 6 små uopfødte, nøgne børn i stor borg og gield..."* — matching the source's own tally of 6 children. Died 1762; his wife Else Pedersdatter died 1768, and the 1769 probate after her is unusually rich: her son Knud (the successor degn, "b.") gave pointed testimony about having alone borne his father's debts, rebuilt the collapsed degnebolig, and supported his mother in old age, "uden at mine Søskende har givet et ringeste dertil" — while three brothers (Peder, Daniel, Hans) sent in written renunciations of any inheritance claim, quoted in full, and a fourth (Mogens) couldn't be found at all.

All 6 children ("a."-"f.") modeled with vitals from the overview table: a. Mogens Knudsen Hopp, b. Knud Knudsen Hopp (successor degn), c. Peder Knudsen Hopp, d. Daniel Knudsen Hopp (degn i Vindum), e. Hans Christian Knudsen Hopp (degn i V. Hornum), f. Else Knudsdatter Hopp (unmarried).

`family-tree/tree.ged`: `#I384`, `#I385`, `#F127` updated; `#I674`-`#I685`, `#F222`-`#F227` new (12 individuals, 6 families).

## Checkpoint 2 — Branch "a." Mogens Knudsen Hopp, full story + 8 children (2026-08-08)

The most dramatic material in the file. Mogens Knudsen Hopp became sognedegn i Jebjerg-Lyby in 1758 by marrying into the post (his wife was the previous degn's daughter). In 1765 he and an itinerant stranger, Nicolai Høeg, were accused of manufacturing and passing counterfeit "kroner" — an assessment of his estate found almost nothing (2 rdr. 5 sk. total), and both men had already fled by the time officials arrived. A formal manhunt followed, with a physical description circulated in a regional bulletin ("gazetterne") noting he'd fled with his 11-year-old son. The case has an unusual resolution: a destitute wandering man died in Rennerlund, Hedensted sogn on 28 July 1765, accompanied by a boy who gave his name as "Peder" and his age as about ten — matching the fugitive's description closely enough that the court accepted it as proof of death, without the body ever being formally identified. The 1767 Landsting verdict found Mogens Hopp guilty in absentia ("bør have forbrudt ære, haand og boes-loed, om hand antræffes og er i live") and acquitted Høeg.

All 8 children (per the source's own overview table) modeled: one unnamed infant death (1752), **Peder Mogensen Hopp** (1753-1823 — the boy who fled with his father and later brought news of the death; married three times, career as husmand/snedker/boelsmand in Bostrup), **Niels Mogensen Hopp** (1757-1774, died age 16), **Christiane Sophia Mogensdatter Hopp** (b.1760, married Niels Mortensen 1780, 9 children), two infant daughters both named Else Kierstine (1761, 1761-1762), **Johanne Helvig Mogensdatter Hopp** (b.1763, presumed dead before 1797), and **Else Mogensdatter Hopp** ("lever 1798" per the overview table — one of only 3 children who reached adulthood, per the source's own count, but with no further detail found; possibly identical to Johanne Helvig given a genuine, unresolved numbering inconsistency between the source's summary table and its later detailed section, flagged rather than silently resolved).

**Capped per policy:** Peder Mogensen Hopp's own descendants run many generations deep into the 20th century — a documented farm diary (1891-1919) kept by a great-grandson, named individuals born as late as 1979 (likely still living) — not modeled. Christiane Sophia's 9 children with Niels Mortensen also not modeled individually (not a short/simple branch).

`family-tree/tree.ged`: `#I686`-`#I697`, `#F228`-`#F231` (12 individuals, 4 families).

## Checkpoint 3 — Branches "b." and "c." (2026-08-08)

Both closed out in the same pass as Checkpoint 2, since the overview had already flagged them as short.

**b. Knud Knudsen Hopp** (`#I676`) succeeded his father directly as degn in Thise (skoleholder from 1747, formally named "successor" 1758, sole degn 1762-1797). Married Dorthe Knudsdatter "Liime" (b.1719, Lime) 1765 in Selde. No children. Praised directly by the provost in a 1789 bishop's visitation report as "en af flid og duelighed udmærket degn." A minor curiosity worth flagging: the parish priest during his tenure, J.H.D.T. Hopp, carried the same surname — KEJ's own source states a connection between the two Hopp lineages "ikke p.t. er lykkedes at påvise" (not yet been possible to demonstrate), i.e. explicitly unconfirmed, not a claimed relationship.

**c. Peder Knudsen Hopp** (`#I678`) held a fæste in Thise 1763-1769, then moved to Skive and later Brøndum. As an 18-year-old in 1754 he was present at (and, per his brother Daniel's testimony, physically intervened in) a documented case of domestic violence by a drunk neighboring degn — recorded in a provosty court transcript quoted at length in the source, though not reproduced in full here since it documents someone else's misconduct, not a Hopp family member's own life. His one known child, **Else Pedersdatter** (`#I698`), married Morten Pedersen in Brøndum and cared for her aunt Dorthe (I677) during her final illness in 1798.
