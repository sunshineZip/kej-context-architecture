Working extraction notes from KEJ's Hopp-slægten Kapitel 1 material — currently two files, "01" (Knud Danielsen Hopp, mostly missing content) and "01C" (Niels Jørgen Knudsen Hopp, 1685-1770, 88 pages). Project-layer working notes, not domain knowledge — no Hopp domain exists yet (Phase 3 not started). Both files stored at `library/deep-wells/hopp-slaegten/`, registered as one growing deep well: `library/reference-index.md#hopp-slaegten`.

**Source citations** (per `authoring-guidelines.md` §9.5, adapted — KEJ's own unpublished, per-chapter files, not a single manuscript):
- Knud Erik Jakobsen (KEJ), Risum, "Hopp, Kapitel 1 — Oversigt / 01 Knud Danielsen Hopp," unpublished, version okt. 2015, received attached to email 2026-08-08. Citation slug used below: `hopp-slaegten, 01-oversigt`.
- Knud Erik Jakobsen (KEJ), Risum, "Hopp, Kapitel 1, Afsnit 01C — Niels Jørgen Knudsen Hopp, 1685-1770," unpublished, version december 2023, received attached to email 2026-08-08. Citation slug used below: `hopp-slaegten, 01C`.

**Methodology:** same as `boe-slaegten-chapter1-extraction.md` — per-claim `[VERIFIED: hopp-slaegten, <slug>]` citations rather than one file-level tag, and consequential atomic facts (dates, named individuals, direct kirkebog/skifte quotations) kept close to verbatim rather than paraphrased, so a misread is visible at a glance against the source. No page-level precision attempted here (same limitation noted in the Boe-slægten notes — the plain-text extraction used doesn't reliably preserve page breaks); cited at file/section level.

**Scope correction (2026-08-08):** the original Phase 1 plan (`projects/archive-digitization/TODO.md`) was "01's surviving overview table + 01C's full 88 pages." Once actually read, 01C turned out to be comparable in total scope to the *entire* Boe-slægten manuscript, not a single chapter of it — Niels Jørgen Knudsen Hopp's own generation is only the first of several the source document goes on to cover in full biographical depth for each of his 6 children, then their children in turn, and so on. This checkpoint covers the root chain (from "01"'s overview) and Niels Jørgen Knudsen Hopp's own generation plus his 6 children (a.-f.) — not their own children, grandchildren, or beyond. See `projects/system/session-log.md` for the revised, multi-checkpoint plan this prompted.

## Checkpoint 1 — Root chain (Kapitel 1 Oversigt) + Niels Jørgen Knudsen Hopp's generation (2026-08-08)

**From "01"'s surviving overview table** (`family-tree/tree.ged#I364`-`#I385`): the direct male-line chain Knud Danielsen Hopp (01) → Daniel Knudsen Hopp (02, =01-a.) → Niels Danielsen Hopp (03, =02-a.) → Daniel Nielsen Hopp (04, =03-a.) → Nicolai Danielsen Hopp (05, =04-c.) → Jacob Daniel Nicolaisen/Hopp (06, =05-d.), whose 13 children include 7 with known descendant lines (07-13, only these named — the other 6 aren't in the material received). Each generation in this chain is modeled with only the vitals the overview table itself gives (name, dates, spouse, occupation) — none of their own chapters are in hand, so no biographical depth beyond that. A stub for Knud Danielsen Hopp's other named child, Knud Knudsen Hopp (01-f.), was added from an *external secondary source* (`external-source-hopp-herredsfoged.md`) as a stopgap, explicitly `[UNVERIFIED]` — his own file (01F) is already stored but deliberately not read yet (Phase 2), and will supersede this stub.

Also from "01"'s prose (not the table): the Hopp surname's likely German/Dutch origin — "Hopp"/"Hoppen" as the Middle/Low German form of "Hopfen" (hops), a "Hoppener" family documented in Flensburg/Slesvig 1377-1452, and the observation that most Hopp-adjacent coats of arms feature a hop plant. Recorded as a `NOTE` on Knud Danielsen Hopp (`#I364`) rather than a separate document — it's KEJ's own manuscript content, not AI-researched background, so it doesn't need the `ai-research` citation pattern used elsewhere in this repo.

**From "01C" itself** (`family-tree/tree.ged#I386`-`#I397`, plus spouses `#I389`, `#I392`, `#I393`, `#I395`): Niels Jørgen Knudsen Hopp (1685-1770) was sognedegn (parish clerk) in Hjerk-Harre for 60 years, 1710 to his death. Married Helle Iversdatter (betrothed 27.6.1713, married 30.7.1713 in Hjerk); she died 1771. Their 6 children:

- **a. Dorthe Nielsdatter Hopp** (b.1715) — married Nicolaj Pedersen Hjardemaal, degn i Thorum (1702-1749). 5 children, not modeled.
- **b. Ove Nielsen Hopp** (b.1717) — moved to Copenhagen by 1770; "descendants presently unknown" per the source itself, no children recorded.
- **c. Berthe Nielsdatter Hopp** (b.ABT 1721, source text reads "1770" — see correction below) — married twice (Christen Christensen Wilstrup, then Anders Lauritzen Væver). 6 children across both marriages, not modeled.
- **d. Knud Nielsen Hopp** (b.1724) — degn i Oddense-Otting from 1749. 10 children, not modeled.
- **e. Anne Nielsdatter Hopp** (b.1726) — died the same year, aged 10 weeks.
- **f. Maren Norup Nielsdatter Hopp** (b.1727) — died 1813, unmarried.

**Correction, not queued (per the human's 2026-08-08 standing preference to accumulate small discrepancies):** the source's own text gives Berthe Nielsdatter Hopp's (`#I391`) birth year as "1770" in one place, but this is chronologically impossible — she's stated elsewhere to have married and had children well before 1770, and the same passage gives her baptism as "1.1.1721." Read as a probable single-digit transcription artifact (1770 for 1721, i.e. a full digit-order scramble rather than the more common single-digit swap seen elsewhere in this repo) rather than a genuine source inconsistency. `tree.ged` uses 1721 (from the explicit baptism date), with the reasoning noted inline rather than escalated.

**Not yet modeled**, deliberately deferred: any of the 6 children's *own* children (the "I.", "II." etc. numbered generation the source goes on to cover for each) — this is where the document's real bulk lives, and is scoped as its own set of checkpoints going forward (see session log).

## Checkpoint 2 — Dorthe Nielsdatter Hopp's child "I." (Anne Cathrine Nicolaisdatter Hjardemaal), full line (2026-08-08)

Per the human's explicit choice (full depth, every generation, no cutoff), read Dorthe Nielsdatter Hopp's (`#I388`) first child's — Anne Cathrine Nicolaisdatter Hjardemaal (`#I398`) — *entire* descendant line, all the way to its leaves. This single sub-branch produced **230 individuals and 75 families** (`#I398`-`#I627`, `#F133`-`#F207`) — nearly 7x Checkpoint 1's size, and roughly a third of 01C's total paragraph count, confirming the scope correction above is, if anything, an understatement.

The line runs continuously from 1740 to the present day: seven children, dozens of grandchildren spread across Denmark and one emigrant branch to the US (1870, Mankato), and several living descendants born as late as the 1990s (e.g. Louise and Jannie Kousgaard Hansen, b.1990/1993 — not modeled individually since the source itself doesn't name a corresponding sub-branch reaching that far on every line, but comparable recency appears elsewhere). **60 of the 230 individuals are presumed-living** (no death date, plausible age) and were redacted to `Living /<surname>/` placeholders per the standing rule, full records moved to `restricted/tree-sensitive.ged`.

Two source-transcription problems noted inline, not queued (per the human's standing preference): a death year "1737" for someone born 1835 (impossible — read as a slip for 1837) and a marriage year "1876" between two people who couldn't have been born yet (read as an unresolved slip, not corrected to a specific guess). One structural ambiguity also noted inline: two individuals (Niels Christian Hansen Ginnerup, Mathilde Hansen Ginnerup) appear in the source immediately after a numbered list without a clear parent statement — modeled as standalone individuals with the ambiguity flagged rather than guessed into a specific family.

Extraction notes are deliberately more compact than Checkpoint 1's style for this checkpoint — core vitals, marriages, and genuinely distinctive facts (an 1828 paternity court case quoted at length, an 1848-50 war record, an 1870 emigration) kept; routine "confirmed with grade X" and duplicate cross-reference mentions trimmed. This was a deliberate pacing choice given the volume, not a change in citation rigor — every claim still carries `[VERIFIED: hopp-slaegten, 01C]`.

**Remaining scope for 01C**, now that Checkpoint 2 gives a real data point: Dorthe Nielsdatter Hopp has 4 more children (II.-V.) not yet touched, at least one of which (IV.) spans roughly as many source paragraphs as child I. did. Niels Jørgen Knudsen Hopp's other children with documented descendants (c. Knud Nielsen Hopp, 10 children; d. — wait, this is Berthe Nielsdatter Hopp per Checkpoint 1's lettering, 6 children across two marriages) are also untouched. Extrapolating from Checkpoint 2's density, 01C alone likely contains somewhere in the range of 800-1500+ individuals at full depth — see `projects/system/session-log.md` Turn 25 for the fuller assessment.
